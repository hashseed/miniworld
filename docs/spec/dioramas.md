# Recurring dioramas

The plan of **recurring dioramas** for every region of the world and every era: the everyday work, trade, travel, festivals and conflicts that happened again and again, such as ice-age hunters stalking a mammoth, farmers sowing in the Fertile Crescent, Viking crews raiding a coast or airliners crossing the oceans. Each diorama is a **repeated vignette** in the sense of [milestone-2.md](milestone-2.md#6-vignettes) section 6: one record covers a span and an area, and its instances appear at dates and sites chosen by hash. Singular vignettes (building the pyramids) and stretched ones (the raid on Lindisfarne) belong to their landmarks and events and are not listed here.

This file is an editorial list like [events.md](events.md): it decides what exists and what happens in it. When a diorama is built, its row becomes a vignette record in `data/curated/vignettes/<id>.md` with the same id, its card text in English and German, and its sources (content-format section 2); the script below becomes the beats of its template, or the record's choice of template parameters. Spans are approximate and are checked against sources when the record is written; every diorama has its title and script in English and German, which are developed together as equal localisations: the record's card text in both languages is written from them.

Decided by Yang on 2026-10-17: every part of the world gets recurring dioramas in every era, each with a small script of what happens. Decisions marked **Proposed** are open for Yang to change; the open questions are in section 15.

## 1. Rules

- **Coverage.** Every regional lane of the timeline ([eras.md](eras.md)) has at least one diorama active at every date at which people live in that region, and the regions with dense records (Europe, the Middle East including Egypt and Persia, South Asia and East Asia) have at least two from 3000 BCE onward (**Proposed**). Dioramas in the World list count for every region their area covers. The coverage report of [content-format.md](content-format.md#8-generated-indexes) section 8 gains a vignette column, so gaps show up in the build.
- **Everyday first.** Most dioramas show how people lived: hunting, farming, building, trading, travelling, learning and celebrating. Conflict appears where it shaped ordinary life for long stretches (Viking raids, the trenches, air raids), never as the main thing a region is shown doing.
- **The cast follows the culture region.** A diorama with a long span, such as the Nile farming year, takes its cast, clothes, tools, houses and boats from the culture profile of its site at the instance's date, unless the record fixes them. So one record shows Old Kingdom farmers in 2500 BCE and Ottoman-era farmers in 1700 at the same work, and morphing (spec section 2.4) carries the people from one era's look to the next.
- **Dioramas end with their way of life.** A diorama's span ends where its activity really ended or changed beyond the script, such as the annual Nile flood ending with the Aswan High Dam around 1970. Where one way of life replaces another, the next diorama in the list starts at the same place.
- **Scripts.** Each script below is the loop of one instance, in three to six beats that use the action vocabulary (milestone-2 section 6.4, with the additions in section 3). The loop must close (milestone-2 section 6.5): people who leave come back or are replaced by newcomers entering the same way. Lasting outcomes, such as a burnt village or a new border, are events, never loops.
- **Presentation.** Spec section 4 applies: nothing bloody, nothing softened. Hunted animals and fallen people sink and fade. Hard history (slavery, forced labour, colonial rule, apartheid, war) is shown as what it was, with the people who suffered it, the people who forced it and its injustice visible in the scene, and explained on the card. Genocide, including the Holocaust, is never a looping diorama (**Proposed**); it is told through events and cards with care (milestones.md, milestone 8).
- **Where instances stand.** The "Where" column is the record's `area`; the "Coordinates" column is one representative point in the format of [content-format.md](content-format.md#4-place) section 4, used to fly to the diorama from its card and from the list of dioramas. Sites inside the area are chosen as in milestone-2 section 6.6.
- **Route dioramas.** Ships, caravans, trains and aircraft travel far beyond a stage. A diorama with the site kind `routes` (milestone-2 section 6.6) places each instance on a route record: the two ends are small stages where people board, load and unload, and between them the vehicle travels the route at a fixed watching speed so that one crossing takes the loop length (**Proposed**: 60 to 120 seconds at ×1, whatever the distance). At orbit zoom the travelling vehicle is the diorama; close up, the stages at the ends are.
- **When they ship.** A diorama ships with the milestone that builds the culture profiles of its region and period ([milestones.md](milestones.md)). Before then its region shows ordinary figures only. The three dioramas of milestone 2 (section 12 of [milestone-2.md](milestone-2.md#12-content-of-this-milestone)) are `mammoth-hunt`, `shore-gathering` and `raft-crossing-sahul` below.

## 3. Additions to the action vocabulary

The scripts need these actions beyond the first set of milestone-2 section 6.4. Each is added with its movement rule and clips when the first diorama that needs it is built.

- Figures: `herd` (drive a group of animals to a mark), `dance`, `bow` (bow down as a procession passes), `row` (with oars, beside `paddle-to`), `duck` (crouch under cover), `play` (as a beat, for children and games).
- Props: `fly-to` (an aircraft or airship takes off, travels along the route at height and lands), `roll-along` (wheeled vehicles and trains along a path), `launch` (rise on a smoke column and vanish), `smoke` (chimney, kiln or furnace smoke without flames), `flood` (a water surface rises over fields and sinks again).

## 15. Review for tone and open questions

These dioramas show hard history; their scripts, scenes and cards are reviewed by Yang for accuracy and tone before they ship, as the milestones require for the conquest of the Americas, the slave trade and the world wars: `atlantic-slave-trade-coast`, `plantation-slavery-americas`, `potosi-silver`, `cash-crops-to-the-coast`, `spice-islands`, `rubber-plantations`, `convict-builders`, `apartheid-township`, `soldiers-forage`, `western-front-trenches`, `air-raids`, `atlantic-convoys`, `ho-chi-minh-trail`, `divided-berlin`, `crusader-castle-siege`, `horse-nomad-raids`, `viking-raids-britain`.

**Open questions for Yang:**

1. Two or more dioramas at every date from 3000 BCE in the regions with dense records, and at least one everywhere people live (section 1).
2. No looping diorama for genocide, including the Holocaust, which is told through events and cards instead (section 1).
3. Route dioramas cross any distance in 60 to 120 seconds of watching time (section 1).
4. Hunted animals fall in view in the hunting dioramas, as milestone-2 section 6.4 already allows; the alternative is to cut away as the spears fly.
