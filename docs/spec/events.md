# Events

Dates come from general historical knowledge, are approximate where marked "c.", and are checked against sources during the data build. Natural disasters, epidemics and famines are listed separately under [Natural disasters, epidemics and famines](#natural-disasters-epidemics-and-famines); the timeline shows both lists together.

## Natural disasters, epidemics and famines

Nature shaped history as much as kings and armies did: ice ages opened land bridges, droughts ended empires, eruptions darkened the sky for years and plagues changed whole continents. This list covers the disasters a child should meet on the globe, from the start of the app at 50,000 BCE to today. Each has an English and a German title.

**How events were chosen.** An event is on the list if at least one of these is true: it changed the course of history (an empire weakened, people moved, a new country formed); it is a record (largest eruption, strongest earthquake ever measured); it led to new knowledge or protection (seismology, sewers, flood barriers); or it is widely taught in English- or German-speaking schools. The list is spread across the world and across time, and is weighted towards events that can be shown clearly on the globe.

**How they are presented.** The rules of [spec.md section 4](spec.md#4-representing-history) apply. The globe shows the force of nature (ash clouds, shaking ground, waves, ice, drought) and its effect on the land and on settlements; people are shown fleeing, sheltering, helping and rebuilding, and lost figures fade out as described there, with nothing graphic. Cards give the number of deaths where it is known, as a number, and say who helped and what people learned. Where people made a disaster worse, through policy, neglect or conquest, the card says so plainly; a famine caused mainly by people is listed in the main event list as a political event, not here.

**Climate shifts** (ice ages, cold spells, droughts, the drying of the Sahara) are slow natural disasters. They appear as changes to the terrain, ice, rivers and fields over their real span rather than as a single moment.

**Considered and left out.** The Toba eruption (about 74,000 years ago) is before the app's start. The Black Sea deluge and Atlantis are contested or legendary and are not shown as events. Helike (373 BCE), Mount Pelée (1902), Messina (1908), Galveston (1900), Hurricane Katrina (2005) and the Haiti earthquake (2010) are important but would crowd the list; they can be added later. Famines caused mainly by people, such as the Holodomor (1932–1933), the Bengal famine (1943) and the Great Chinese Famine (1959–1961), are not natural disasters and belong in the main event list.
