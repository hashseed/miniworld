# Fields and crops

Farming villages of the Neolithic are drawn with fields around the lodgings: organic patches of tilled ground, each planted with a crop that grows, ripens, is cut and lies bare again through the year. This file specifies the crop models (part A), the pure rule that picks a crop's stage from the date (part B), the geometry of the fields (part C) and how they are drawn on the terrain (part D). Status: **Proposed**, written 2026-10-23; not approved by Yang. Models are generated by scripts and wait in the asset manifest with status `generated` (milestone 2 section 13.2).

## 1. Crop models (part A)

Each crop is a small clay tuft, a few plants standing together, built by `assets/src/crops.ts` (`cropGeometry(id, stage)`) in the style of the plant models of [foliage.md](foliage.md) section 5: chunky flat-coloured shapes with vertex colours, cel-shaded by the same light as the terrain, **no ink outline** (a tuft is too small for one, and the foliage has none). Y is up and the tuft's footprint is a square one unit wide, centred on the origin: one unit is one planting spacing, so tufts placed one unit apart in a grid tile into a continuous crop with no gap and no overlap. The renderer scales the whole tuft by the spacing it wants.

Which crops a village grows is decided by its region and date, outside this file; the field layout only receives the list.

### 1.1 Growth stages

A tuft is built for a **stage** `s` from 0 to 1. Five keyframes are named:

| Stage | `s` | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bare | 0 | Tilled soil only: two or three low clods, no plant (rice: wet mud) |
| Sprout | 0.25 | Short thin blades, pale fresh green |
| Green | 0.5 | Full height, deep green, leaves out, no ears or fruit yet |
| Ripe | 0.75 | Full height, golden (or the crop's ripe colour), ears, cobs and fruit on |
| Stubble | 1 | Cut stalks about 0.12 high, straw colour, fruit gone |

Heights, fruit size and colours are interpolated between keyframes, so any `s` is a valid tuft. A model is approved at its ripe stage (`crop-<id>`, `s = 0.75`).

### 1.2 Triangle budget and instancing plan

- The ripe counts in the table are measured. Maize and banana leaves overhang the unit footprint (arching leaves reach about 0.6 beyond it), so their spacing leaves room for that.
- A tuft is **40 to 120 triangles**, at most 120 at any stage; the bare tuft is under 20. Blades and stalks are thin open pyramids of three sides (3 triangles), ears four-sided pyramids, leaves flat fans. No tuft has a closed bottom.
- Thousands are drawn, so: one `InstancedMesh` per **crop and stage keyframe** (13 crops times 5 keyframes, at most 65 draw calls, in practice fewer than 15 because a camp grows one to three crops at a time and a field is in one keyframe at a time). A field's tufts are all in the keyframe nearest its stage; the continuous part of the stage (height between two keyframes) is the instance matrix's Y scale, so a growing crop rises smoothly without a rebuild.
- Tufts stand on a jittered grid inside the field outline: spacing 0.11 layout metres for cereals, flax and lentil, 0.2 for millet, rice, maize, squash, potato, quinoa and taro, 0.4 for banana (a layout metre is about 24 drawn km, so these are still tufts of a whole stretch of field, like the trees). A field of 0.9 metres across therefore holds about 40 to 100 tufts, 5 fields about 2,500 at most, 300,000 triangles at the very most per village in full view; a village seen from orbit drops tufts (draws every second, then every fourth) and relies on the ground patch of part D alone. The grid, its jitter and the turn of each tuft come from `hash32` of the field's seed and the cell, never from `Math.random`.
- Draping: the instance is placed on the field's draped ground height like the ground patch, so tufts follow the terrain.

## 2. The stage of a crop on a day (part B)

**A field changes only when somebody works it (Yang, 2026-10-24: "crops grow too quickly. they should grow much slower, and I want activities in the field like plowing and harvest etc. fields that are not harvested or plowed should not continue to grow").** The old calendar (`cropCalendar.ts`, a crop's stage from the day of the year) is gone: at Neolithic dates a year passes in a few seconds of watching, so the crops flickered through their whole cycle. The stage is now a **pure function of the village's life clock**, `fieldStage(clock, field, ms)` in `app/src/tree/fieldWork.ts`. No `Math.random`, no wall-clock time.

**States.** A crop cycle has six states: stubble (stage 1), tilled earth (0), seed (0.1), sprouts (0.35), green (0.55), ripe (0.75). Each is left by one job: `till` (stubble to tilled), `sow`, `weed` three times, then `reap` (ripe to stubble). The next job of a field is `CYCLE[state]`. Each field starts in a state taken from the village's hash, so the fields of a village are not all alike.

**Slots.** The village gives out one job per slot of `JOB_MS` = 30 s of the clock, to the fields in turn (the first field set by the village's hash). A field therefore changes once in every `n` slots (`n` the number of fields) and holds still in between; a field nobody works does not grow. The result shows `CHANGE_MS` = 26 s into the slot, easing over `EASE_MS` = 4 s (a cut, no ease, from stubble to tilled earth). Between two jobs the stage is constant. A full cycle of one field takes six jobs, so several minutes of watching with a few fields.

**Who works.** The job of a slot goes to one **assigned worker**: a grown-up farmer or reaper who is at the camp at the slot's start, chosen by hash among the ids in ascending order, whose turn contains the slot start. Their `till`, `sow` or `reap` outing (`weed` is drawn as the till clip) is at that field; they walk there, work and walk back as in section 3. The other farmers' extra field work (the share of the profile's activity mix) goes to the field's next job, so several people work one field. If no farmer is at the camp at a slot (all away hunting or trading), nobody works, but the arithmetic still counts the job: an accepted approximation, as the field's stage must not depend on who happens to be home.

**Water.** Rice paddies stand in water (wet mud when bare) until the harvest and are drained for the stubble: part D draws water in a field of crop `rice` while `s < 0.99`.

## 3. Field shapes (part C)

`app/src/tree/fieldShapes.ts` is a pure, deterministic module in layout metres (x east, z north, one layout metre about 24 drawn km, `LAYOUT_KM_PER_M`). `layoutFields(input)` returns the fields of one village.

**Inputs.** The village centre, the outer radius of its ring of lodgings, a seed, the list of crops the village grows, a land test (`isLand(x, z)`, true at dry level and on gentle slope; the caller combines the sea of the date, slope and height), a blocked test (`isBlocked(x, z)`, true on rivers, lakes, mountains and anything else fields must avoid), and the most fields wanted (default 10, at most 10). Land and blocked tests are plain functions so the module has no dependency on the globe: the caller builds them from `SurfaceSource` and the river/sea fields of the tree layer.

**Rules.**

1. **Count.** Not many, but large (Yang, 2026-10-24: "Not too many fields, but slightly larger ones"): a village wants one more field than it has lodgings, **2 to 5**, as many as the free space allows. Where land is short (a coast, a gorge, a river bend between mountains) the count is lower, even below 3 or none at all: fewer fields, never forced fields (Yang's decision). A field must also fit, so a gap too small for a decent field gets none.
2. **Placement.** Candidate sites lie on a hashed jittered spiral in the band from the lodging ring (plus margin) to 4.5 layout metres beyond it, nearer sites first (the nearest edge of a field is about 14 m from the hearth, "much closer to the living quarters", Yang 2026-10-24) (with hashed noise, so the order differs by seed). A site is taken if the land is free around it (at least six of eight points at 0.7 m), at least 1.5 m from the other sites.
3. **Cells.** The sites are relaxed (two rounds of Lloyd's centroid step) in a **power diagram** with a small hashed weight per site, so cells differ in size; each cell is the intersection of the bisector half-planes, bounded by a regular 24-gon of radius 0.85 m around its site (0.62 m until 2026-10-24, so a field has almost twice the area). Cells are convex and never overlap. The cell is then **inset by 0.09 m** (leaving a path between neighbours), **Chaikin-smoothed** (two rounds of corner cutting) and given a **low-frequency edge wobble**: the radius from the cell's centroid is multiplied by a smooth factor between 0.86 and 1 that depends on the angle (two sine waves of hashed phase, so only a few bulges per field). The wobble only pulls inwards and the cell is convex, so the result stays inside its cell: fields never overlap.
4. **Never square.** The result is a closed outline of 30 to 60 points, rounded and uneven; the ratio of its area to that of its smallest bounding rectangle is below 0.9 for every field (a rectangle's is 1), which the tests check.
5. **Exclusions.** Every outline vertex, and each of four points 0.08 m (margin) around it, must be land, not blocked and outside the lodging ring plus the margin; points along every edge (every 0.05 m) and in the interior (every 0.2 m) are tested too. A failing vertex is pulled towards the centroid (still inside the cell) until it passes; if an edge or the interior still fails, the whole field shrinks towards its centroid by 15% up to six times; if it still fails, or its area ends below 0.16 m² (about 0.4 m across), the field is dropped.
6. **Furrow direction.** The long axis of the outline plus a hashed turn of up to ±0.3 radians, as an angle in the x–z plane in radians (0 = east). Furrows run along it.
7. **Crop.** Chosen from the village's list by rotating through it from a hashed start, so neighbouring fields differ when the list has more than one crop; `fieldOffsetDays` is 0 to 14 from the hash.
8. **Determinism.** Everything comes from `hash32` of the seed and indices and from `detSin`/`detCos`/`detAtan2`; the same input gives the same fields on every device and run. The module imports no globe or three.js code.

**Output.** `Field { id, crop, outline (closed, counter-clockwise, first point not repeated), centre, area (m²), furrowAngle, offsetDays }`. Fields are sorted nearest to the village first, so a caller showing fewer fields (a small village) takes a prefix.

**Scale.** A field is about 0.6 to 1.2 layout metres across (14 to 29 km on the globe), compact on purpose: crops are drawn big to be recognised, not to scale (Yang, 2026-10-23). A village in the dioramas has the 2 to 6 lodgings of its ring; the ring radius is the caller's.

## 4. Drawing the ground of a field (part D)

`app/src/tree/fieldGround.ts` follows `campGround.ts`: a ground patch, triangulated from the outline, draped on the drawn terrain.

- **Triangulation.** The outline is star-shaped about its centroid (part C), so a fan from the centroid works: five rings (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1 of the radius) of the outline's own points give a draped patch of about five triangles per outline point. Each vertex is lifted onto `SurfaceSource.surfaceKm` plus a lift of 4 km (just enough to clear the drawn terrain; at 22 km the patches hovered over sloping ground, Yang 2026-10-24).
- **Layout to globe.** `add(origin, field, stage)` takes the village's drawn position on the globe and places layout metres with `northEast` and `LAYOUT_KM_PER_M`, as the camps do; the patch is cached by place and field and rebuilt only when `groundVersion` changes.
- **Shader.** One `ShaderMaterial`, `DoubleSide`, transparent, `depthWrite` off, `polygonOffset` -2, the log-depth chunks of the globe, `frustumCulled` off, `renderOrder` 1. Attributes per vertex: the position in the field's furrow frame (metres across and along the furrows), the distance from the rim (0 at the rim, 1 inside), the stage `s`, the crop's green and ripe colours (a table in the file, matching part A) and a water flag. The fragment shader paints: soil brown with **furrow stripes** (alternate light and dark bands across the furrow frame, one band per 0.07 m, softened with `fwidth` so they fade into plain colour before they shimmer), the crop colour in the stripes growing with `s` (sprout pale green thin stripes at 0.25, deep green at 0.5, gold at 0.75, straw at 1, and bare soil at 0), and no clear boundary (Yang, 2026-10-24: "fields should not have that clear boundaries"): no dark bund, and the earth fades into the ground over the outer 1.5 layout metres, ragged by a low-frequency noise on the furrow frame; the tufts thin out towards the rim too (half are left out in the outer fifth and those kept are drawn at three quarters of the size). Rice shows still blue-grey water between the stripes while the water flag is set. The stage per field is an attribute rather than a uniform because the fields of all villages share one draw call; it changes only when the day crosses a stage step (the patch is rewritten, not rebuilt).
- **Visibility.** The patches are drawn at every altitude (Yang, 2026-10-24: "fields disappear when zooming out"); the camp patch fades out with nearness, a field never fades. From 6,000 km of camera altitude the vertex shader enlarges each field about its own centre, along the sphere, by altitude / 6,000 (at most 4 times), so a field stays a few pixels wide from orbit, as a flat patch of its stage colour with the ragged edge; the furrows have already blended into plain colour by then. No extra triangles: the same patches, one draw call. The tufts of part A take over close up and are gone beyond 6,000 km.
- **API.** `new FieldGround(surface)`, then each frame `begin()`, `add(origin: THREE.Vector3, field: Field, stage: number)` for every visible field, `end(cameraKm, groundVersion)`; `mesh` is added to the scene once. `FieldGround` draws only the ground; the crop tufts are instanced separately (section 1.2) by the tree layer, using the same outline.

## 5. Tests

- `fieldShapes.test.ts`: count between 3 and 10 in open country, fewer on a strip of land, none in a gorge; no two outlines overlap; no outline contains or touches a banned point (river polyline, mountain disc, sea half-plane, the lodging ring); never rectangular (area over smallest bounding rectangle below 0.9); about 0.6 to 1.2 metres across; the same seed gives identical output, a different seed a different layout.
- `fieldWork.test.ts`: slots go to the fields in turn; a field changes by exactly one state, once in every n slots, and only after `CHANGE_MS`; `jobAt` is `CYCLE[state]`; the stage is constant between jobs.
- `crops` in `assets/src/models.test.ts`: every crop at every keyframe builds the same geometry twice and stays under 120 triangles; each `crop-<id>` is in the manifest.
