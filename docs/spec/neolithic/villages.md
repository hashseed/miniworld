# Neolithic villages that stay

How a farming village of the tree of humankind is placed, laid out and lives, replacing the hunter-gatherer camp rules where they no longer fit. Status: approved by Yang on 2026-10-25 with the answers recorded below; built part by part. It answers Yang's list of 2026-10-25:

> villages in the neolithic no longer move. they stay. So let's make sure they are in locations of future cities
>
> long houses in europe are way too small. they need to be twice as large. ... long houses don't need to fit into any ring. there is no ring around a fire place in the neolithic anymore!
>
> once bands move into neolithic society, they should no longer form circles with their houses now that the fireplace is gone. they should also no longer build stone tools. ... villages in the neolithic should spend time making tools, not knapping stones in a primitive way. ... villagers in the neolithic no longer gather berries or roots from the vicinity. they work the fields.
>
> a village has way too many people in the neolithic. let's have half as many.
>
> sometimes houses are built over a cliff. ... fields should not be under trees. fields should not be under houses. animal enclosures should not include trees. ... there are no farm animals in the enclosures. there are no oxens pulling plows.
>
> the year dates are not accurate. there is a sign for yellow river farming at 6500 BCE but the first fields don't appear until 6018 BCE. ... ensure anchors for every sign of entering farming age!

The parts are numbered so they can be built and reviewed one at a time, each spec first, in this order: 1 (anchors), 2 (village sites), 3 (staying), 4 (people), 5 (layout), 6 (ground rules), 7 (herds and oxen), 8 (work).

## 1. Every farming sign is an anchor

Each threshold of `data/curated/neolithic.json` (a farming centre, its year and point; the sign of [../humankind.md](../humankind.md) section 8.4 and the event of farming-spread.md section 9.5) is also an anchor of [../sites.md](../sites.md) section 4.1, made by the build from the threshold itself, so the date and place are written once:

- **Built 2026-10-25.** The planning pass brings a band to the threshold's site by its year, as for every threshold (peopling.md section 3.6), and **on the day the band settles there it becomes a village of the profile whose area holds the threshold's point** (`thresholdCulture`, `farmingProfileAt`) and builds its farming houses with their fields: no morph delay (elsewhere up to 100 years, `MORPH_YEARS`), and whatever the farming grid says at its site (it gives the centre's date only within `CENTRE_CORE_KM` = 100 km of the point; the Yellow River's site is 124 km from Cishan, so until now its fields began in 6016 BCE, not 6500).
- **Checked.** The build fails when a threshold whose point lies in a farming profile's area has no farming house with fields at its site, or its first is built more than 50 years after the threshold's year (`FARMING_LATE_YEARS`), naming the centre and the place; it logs how many years after its date each centre's first fields stand (on 2026-10-25: 2 to 9 years). The sign and the first fields can no longer disagree.
- **The sign shows when it happens in miniworld** (Yang, 2026-10-25: "show the actual time when things take place in miniworld"). The sign's and the event's day is the day the first farming house with fields is built at the threshold's site in the built tree, read from the tree by the data build, not copied from the threshold's year; the threshold's year is what the tree aims for. The existing anchors at farming centres (`jericho`, `nanchoc`, `mehrgarh`, `kuk`, and `catalhoyuk` for the Konya plain) keep their scenes and texts; where their year differs from their centre's threshold (today `jericho` 8000 BCE against the Fertile Crescent's 9500, `nanchoc` 8000 against the Andes' 3500, `kuk` 8000 against New Guinea's 5000), the threshold's year is the farming village's and the anchor's own year stays the year its band arrives.
- Every centre's threshold is such an anchor, so Amazonia, Mesoamerica, the Yellow River and the Yangtze, which had no anchor, are held like the others.

## 2. Village sites are the places of future cities

**Built 2026-10-25**: every city of the extract that lies in a farming profile's area (`farmingProfileAt`) asks for a camp site within 150 km of it (`VILLAGE_SITE_KM`), laid as a key place's is, and the site nearest it within that distance is a **village site**. The farming grid gives a site its profile only at a village site, so a band elsewhere in farming land stays hunter-gatherers until it stands at one, and a village buds only to village sites. A threshold's band farms at its own site (part 1). On the real build: 276 village sites for 696 places of future cities in farming land; every one of the 18 villages stands within 25 to 125 km of a future city (Konya, Plovdiv, Shaoxing, Oaxaca, Elephantine and others), drawn on it at the camps' scale, but for the thresholds' own villages in the Andes and New Guinea, which the curated list is for.

**The curated list and the founding walk (built 2026-10-25).** `data/curated/village-sites.json` lists ten excavated LBK villages (Schwanfeld, Brunn am Gebirge, Bylany, Olszanica, Zwenkau, Langweiler 8, Vaihingen an der Enz, Elsloo, Vráble, Cuiry-lès-Chaudardes), each with its date and source, laid as the cities are. A band whose land the farming spread has reached but which stands off a village site walks, at the end of its stay, to the nearest free village site within five links and 600 km (`VILLAGE_WALK_KM`) and founds its village there. Farmland feeds more people: at a village site that farming has reached, villages keep clear only of each other's drawn camps (their footprints), not their density's spacing (900 km at `low`), which had left room for about four camps in the whole LBK area. On the real build: 38 villages, 13 of them LBK, founded from 6104 to 5163 BCE as the culture spread, six on the Yellow River, five in the Levant, three on the Yangtze.

A farming village is founded only at a **village site**, a dated place of the place table (sites.md section 2.1) where a city later stood, so the villages of 7000 BCE stand where Jericho, Uruk, Thebes, Xi'an or Teotihuacan will grow. The sites come from a source extract under `data/sources/`:

- **Source (Proposed):** Reba, M., Reitsma, F. and Seto, K. C. 2016, *Spatializing 6,000 years of global urbanization from 3700 BC to AD 2000*, Scientific Data 3, 160034, its dataset of geocoded historical cities with their first dated population (from Chandler 1987 and Modelski 2003), licence CC BY 4.0 as published (to be checked on the dataset's own page before the extract is added), which the data licence policy allows (spec section 13). The extract (`data/sources/reba-2016/cities.json`, added 2026-10-25) keeps every geocoded city once with its name, country, position, certainty and the year and size of its first dated population: 1,726 cities, from Eridu (3700 BCE) on.
- Plus the farming anchors of part 1 and the Neolithic sites the spec already names (Çatalhöyük, Mehrgarh, Kuk), so the first villages stand where archaeology finds them.
- Plus a **curated list** (`data/curated/village-sites.json`, Yang, 2026-10-25) for regions that had villages but few later cities, such as the LBK of central Europe and the New Guinea highlands: excavated Neolithic villages with their position, dates and source (for the LBK, for example, Elsloo, Langweiler 8, Bylany, Vaihingen), each a village site like the cities.
- In a farming area, the lattice sites (sites.md section 2) are not used for founding villages; a band that turns farmer at a lattice site walks to the nearest free village site within the culture area's spacing and founds its village there, or stays a hunter-gatherer band if there is none.

## 3. Villages stay

**Built 2026-10-25** (the founding at village sites follows with part 2): a camp of a farming profile does not move at the end of its residence window, as a settled band does not; unlike one, it still buds, also near a threshold. On the real build every farming village stands at one place, most to the end of the tree. As it stays for millennia while the sea rises, it rebuilds its houses in place, as a camp does when the sea reaches its huts or lanes, also when the sea takes land its fields may use (its houses' `fieldLand` mask at the present sea), so its fields are laid out again on land.

From its founding a village does not move: its band is settled at its site (as an anchor's band is, sites.md section 4.1) for as long as the culture area has people, its houses are rebuilt in place, and it buds off new villages by sending a small group (two to four grown-ups and their children) walking to the nearest free village site, never by moving the village. A village is abandoned only where the sea floods its site (it then moves to the nearest free village site inland) or a listed event ends it. The tree's checks add: a farming house is never taken down while its village stands, and no village's site changes.

### 3.1 Tight villages and their own land

Yang, 2026-10-26 ("villages are more compact (houses closer) in tighter areas"), so that more villages fit where farmland is dense, such as along the Euphrates and the Tigris: until then a village kept its full drawn footprint (18 layout metres round its middle, sites.md section 2) clear of every other village's, and the few villages near Urfa, the Zagros and Basra filled the plain.

- **Tight where the full one does not fit.** A village is founded at a village site with the full layout where its footprint stays clear of every other village's; where it does not but the compact layout's does (13.6 layout metres, the layout of a compact camp, sites.md section 2.1), it is founded **tight**: its camp has the compact layout, so its ring, its people's spots and its houses stand closer to its middle (the houses at the share of the compact ring, as a compact camp's are, humankind.md section 8.5), and it holds at most the compact layout's eight people. A village keeps its layout for its whole life. Villages still keep clear of hunter-gatherer bands' footprints as before.
- **Its own land.** Each village's farmland lies only on its own side of the line halfway between it and every other village standing at any time in its life (in true kilometres, each village's middle as the place table lays it): no plot of one village lies nearer another village's middle. The data build works this out once the tree has grown and gives each of the village's lodgings the share as a second land mask (`fieldShare`, cells as in the land mask of section 6), which the fields, the pen and the store keep to and the houses do not, with 2 layout metres to spare either side of the line. So the farmland of two close villages meets at a line between them and never overlaps, and a village's plots stay as they were laid out from its founding.

## 4. People

**Built 2026-10-25.** A village holds **6 to 10 figures** (`VILLAGE_MIN` 6 plus a hash of its id below 5; 12 to 20 until 2026-10-25), in two or three houses. Its fields' jobs need one worker at a time (fields-and-crops.md section 2), which a village of 6 always has at home.

## 5. Layout: rows, not a ring

**Built 2026-10-25.** Each farming profile names its `layout` and, where it builds larger, its `houseM` in the culture table (`cultures.json`, farming block); `villageHouse` (`world/scene/camp.ts`) places a village's houses by it, in layout metres from the village's middle, beyond a green of 9.5 m where the people's spots are: `rows` (the LBK) **one longhouse** north-east of the green, its long side to it and its long axis north-west to south-east, holding the whole village (`houseCapacity` 10; a second place behind it, a metre off, for its next while it is rebuilt), drawn up to 7.56 figure metres long (`houseM`, 30% shorter than the 10.8 before; Yang, 2026-10-30: "the long houses are huuuuge make them smaller by 30%, and there only needs to be one per village"; 2026-10-25: "they need to be twice the size and only one per village is sufficient", after 5.4). **Never more than one house stands**: a village whose house holds it all (`houseCapacity`) builds no second, takes in no more people than the house holds, and moves anyone still in a second house into the first (on the real build 7 of 13 LBK villages had two or three, which piled onto the layout's two places); `stilts` (the Yangtze) the same in a row turned by the camp's hash; `cluster` (Anatolia) a block of three by two wall to wall; `hamlet` (the others) round the yard at 12 to 16 m for a house of 2.7 m (larger houses farther out, by their length, and far enough that five stand side by side even at their inner ends; turned less off their places the larger they are), a little off any ring; `pits` (the Yellow River) keeps the ring. The pre-pottery Neolithic houses of the Levant and the Zagros are drawn at 4.05 m too (half as large again; Yang, 2026-10-30: "these houses in persia are too small, make them 50% larger"). The Yellow River's and the Yangtze's houses are drawn at 4.05 m (`houseM`, half as large again as 2.7; Yang, 2026-10-25: "chinese houses ... are also too small. increase size by 50% each"). A house stands only on land of its land mask that people can walk to, not on a cliff (the mask's relief limit); where any place of the layout fails, the whole village keeps the ring, so the two never mix. Doors face the green; builders gather on the door side. Fields and the pen keep 2 m off every house and out of its shadow from the green, so no walk out to them passes through a house (section 6). A test lays out every profile's village and checks that no two houses overlap and all stand clear of the green.

A village has no fire and no ring of lodgings (sections 8.1 and 8.5 of humankind.md stay for hunter-gatherer and herding camps):

- **A layout per culture** (Yang, 2026-10-25: "different and matching each culture"), each profile naming its layout in the culture table, all of them without a fire ring, no two houses touching whatever their size:
  - `rows` (LBK): longhouses side by side in loose rows, all turned north-west to south-east as the excavated longhouses (Coudart 1998, *Architecture et société néolithique*), a house's width plus 3 layout metres apart.
  - `cluster` (Anatolia, Çatalhöyük): houses packed wall to wall in a block, entered from the roofs by ladders, open yards between blocks (Hodder 2006).
  - `hamlet` (Levant, Mehrgarh, the Nile, Mesoamerica, the Andes, New Guinea): houses scattered round shared yards with storage, a few metres apart, turned towards the yard.
  - `pits` (the Yellow River, Banpo): round pit houses in a ring round a central open space, inside a ditch (Banpo, c. 4800 BCE).
  - `stilts` (the Yangtze, Hemudu): long stilt houses in a row along water (Hemudu, c. 5000 BCE).
  The layouts' measures are set when each is built and shown to Yang.
- **House sizes.** A house is drawn at the size its profile gives, no longer capped by a ring (`LODGING_MAX_M` stays for the camps): the LBK longhouse 7.56 figure metres, one to a village; the Chinese and the pre-pottery Levant houses 4.05; the others at their present size until Yang asks otherwise.
- **The green and the spots.** Each figure's spot is by its own house's door, and the lane between the rows is the village's open ground, where the dances and games of the camp events take place.
- **Fields and pen.** The fields lie beyond the ends of the rows and behind them, the pen at one end of the lane; outings go out along the lane.

## 6. Ground rules, checked by the build

Each is a rule of the village's layout, checked for every village of the built tree, failing the build with the village and the rule:

- **No house over a cliff** (built 2026-10-25 for the village layouts: a house stands only on its land mask's land, whose cells keep within 300 m of the hearth's height; section 5). More strictly, proposed: every house's footprint lies on ground whose drawn height varies by less than a set amount across it (Proposed: the height a figure is tall), and no house's footprint reaches water.
- **No field under a house** and no field under the pen: fields keep a margin from every house's and the pen's footprint (built 2026-10-25: 2 m from every house and out of its shadow from the green, 8 m from the pen's middle).
- **No walk across water to a field or the pen** (built 2026-10-25): a cell of a house's land mask is land only where the straight way out to it from the hearth is land too, checked every half layout metre in the build (the cells are too coarse to see a narrow strait), and a field's or the pen's ground counts as free only where it and the way out to it are land (`maskReach`), as the camps' outing lanes are. A village by a bay had put fields across it, and its farmers walked through the sea. A reaper carries the sheaf back the way it came, to inside the ring, before crossing to the threshing floor. Everyone walks out to a field or an animal of the pen along that straight way, from a point 3 m out from the hearth, and back the same way: a milker who crossed straight from the pen to the grinding stone walked through the sea off a village of the Jordan valley.
- **No tree in a field or an enclosure.** The trees keep off every field and the pen with a margin, from the village's founding (fields-and-crops.md section 2).

## 7. Herds and oxen

**Built 2026-10-25**: the herd's three to six animals stand within 3.8 layout metres of the pen's middle (`HERD_IN_PEN_M`), inside the fence, not 8 to 12 metres round it as before; the pen has a clearing of 11 layout metres round its middle (its fence is about 6 out, and a tree's crown is wide), so no tree stands in it (section 6). A villager tilling where the profile's field tool is the ard on the day (`fieldToolOf`) and the herd has cattle walks behind the ard empty-handed, with two of the village's cattle yoked in front (`ploughTeam`), all at the herd's scale; the till clip stands in place, so the oxen stand with it rather than walk on the spot, and the herd in the pen keeps its count. Animals that lead the ard along the furrow, and a till clip that walks it, are to follow with the model review loop.

- **In the enclosure.** The village's herd stands inside its pen, three to six animals at the herd scale, grazing there; a herder takes them out to graze nearby and back. The herd animal models and every other model then waiting for review were approved by Yang on 2026-10-25 ("approve all assets for now"), so the public build draws them.
- **The pen on the ground** (built 2026-10-25; Yang: "animal pen in the lombardy doesn't work for the terrain. can we make the pen adjust to the terrain?"). The pen is laid on the drawn ground under its fence: the renderer looks the ground up at twelve points round the fence's ring, turns the pen to the plane that best fits them and lowers it until that plane is nowhere above the ground, so on a slope the fence leans with the hill and no part of it floats; where the ground is not flat its foot sinks into the high side.
- **The dog** (built 2026-10-25; Yang: "dog not in the animal pen but follow humans around"). A dog is no herd animal: where the profile's herd lists `animal-dog`, the pen holds the others (a village whose only animal is the dog has no pen) and the village's one dog follows one grown-up at home, the one of lowest hash, so it keeps to one person until they leave. It is drawn where its person was 0.7 seconds before, on their own way, so it never walks where they did not; standing, it stands 1.1 layout metres to their side, easing between the two by how far they went in the last 0.9 seconds, idle or walking as they are.
- **Oxen at the plough.** Where the village's profile has cattle in its herd and the date is past its `ardFromBce`, ploughing is done with the ard drawn by a pair of oxen under a yoke (`prop-ard`, `prop-yoke`, both `generated`): two of the village's cattle walk the furrows of the field being ploughed, the ploughman behind them, and return to the pen. Before that date, and where there are no cattle, ploughing is by hand with the profile's field tool.

## 8. Work

**Built 2026-10-25**: a villager's rest at home has tool-making (the `grind` clip: grinding and polishing a stone axe on a slab) where a hunter-gatherer knaps; a villager's gathering, firewood and stroll outings, and the hunters' spear-making, become field work, the next job of a field of the village (`JOB_OUTING`), or a rest where the village has no field. The `polish` clip below stays to be modelled and reviewed; until then the grinding clip stands for it.

A villager's day has no knapping, gathering or firewood outing:

- **Making tools** replaces knapping: grinding and polishing a stone axe or adze on a grinding slab (the polished stone tools of the Neolithic), and hafting it, at a spot by the house; a new clip, `polish`, to be modelled and reviewed (milestone-2 section 13.5).
- **The grain store** (built 2026-10-25; Yang: "grains are stored in pots or the granary"). Each profile that grows grain names its `store` in the culture table: a raised granary (`prop-granary`) for the Levant (the granaries of Dhra', c. 9300 BCE), the Indus (Mehrgarh's storage buildings) and the Yangtze (raised stores as at Hemudu); a clay-lined storage pit (`prop-silo-pit`) for Anatolia, the Nile (the basket-lined silos of the Fayum), the Yellow River (the pits of Cishan) and Mesoamerica; three large storage jars (`prop-pot-large`) for the LBK and the Andes; none in New Guinea, whose taro and bananas are not stored as grain. It stands 15 layout metres out in the first outing lane clear of the houses and the pen where the land mask shows land there and 2 m to either side (so the way out to it is land too; at 11 m, short of the mask, one stood in the sea off the Lebanese coast), its door towards the village, at 0.6 of life size (the jars at life size), from the first ploughing on, with a clearing of 4 m round it; the fields keep 4 m from it. After threshing, the thresher carries the grain to it, in towards the middle, out along the store's lane to its door, puts it away (`work`, 0.8 seconds), and walks back the same way.
- **Field work** replaces gathering: the farmers' and reapers' extra work at the fields (fields-and-crops.md section 2) takes the turns that went to gathering, and the share of the activity mix that was gathering goes to field work.
- Fetching water, the profile's crafts (pottery, spinning, grinding grain), herding and milking stay; hunting stays at the profile's small hunter share.
