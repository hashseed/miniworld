# Famous people

The first list of 180 famous people, 20 in each category, spread across the world and across history, grouped by category and in order of birth within each category. Each appears as a unique figure at the listed places during the active part of their life and fades out when it ends. Names are the English forms; every name is localised. Dates are approximate where marked "c." and are checked against sources during the data build.

Each person has a stable id, made from the English name in lower case with hyphens, which other records use to link to them and which never changes once published. Wars and battles link to the people who took part in them ([wars.md](wars.md)); the person's card lists those wars and battles, and selecting one flies the camera there.

People still alive at the end of the timeline on 26 December 1991 stay on the globe until it ends. Their card may say in a sentence what they did later, such as Nelson Mandela becoming South Africa's president in 1994.

## Categories

Every person has exactly one main category: the reason they are remembered, which is what a child should learn first. A person may also have one further category when a second side of their life is just as well known, such as Leonardo da Vinci, who was a painter and also an inventor. The categories are used in three places:

- The "Famous people" list can be filtered by category, and search results show each person's category.
- The person's card names the main category under their name ("Rulers", "Herrscherinnen und Herrscher").
- Each category has its own small symbol, shown on the card and in the list; the symbols are generated assets like any other.

The category says what someone did, not whether it was good. A conqueror's card explains the cost of the conquest, and Adolf Hitler is listed with politicians as a dictator, never with anything that sounds like praise.
